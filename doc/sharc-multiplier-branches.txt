Two-clock multiplier flag branch interlocks

Primary evidence: Programming Reference rev2.4,4-115/116/Table4-55 and case2,
9-32/34,9-44/46,10-33. Case2 adds one Decode hold to the ordinary condition
interlock when the immediately preceding compute uses the multiplier and the
branch consumes a multiplier flag. Architectural flag availability after
retirement does not remove the second hold. Manuals remain local; provenance
is in reference/sharc.json. No private implementation is a build input.

The bound sequencer qualifies the implemented normal/positive-zero floating
multiplier's structural dependencies on MV/MS and their complements. Direct
absolute/relative JUMP, CALL and ordinary RTS retain their shared Address
decisions, native stack effects, immediate suppression and real DB slots.
Arithmetic status still follows sharc-floating-multiply.txt; fixed multiply,
exception outcomes and complete operand forwarding remain unqualified.

First hold: F1/F2/Decode, cursor and suppression stay unchanged; the actual
Address producer enters Execute and Address becomes absent. Successful native
retirement updates the result/flags. Flow retains multiplier_branch_wait. A
failed producer keeps Execute and the wait; no later clock can consume it or
silently complete its effects.

Second hold: the retained Decode branch still does not resolve. No instruction
enters Execute, no guest NOP executes and the wait clears with the whole clock
commit. Supported quiet effects/ownership use the existing bound clock. The
following advancing clock selects the real condition/target in Address.
Pending MODE1, PM/cache conflicts and mixed loop stalls retain their guards.
Failed clocks preserve CPU/cache/transport and the pending wait.

An intervening guest instruction removes this immediate-producer dependency.
A branch on retained ALU flags after MUL has no multiplier stall. Ordinary
ALU/shifter/BTF producers retain one-clock interlocks. Conditional post-modify,
floating-to-fixed compute stalls, dual-operand MUL forwarding and general
combinations of dependencies remain separate unfinished work.

Typed snapshots preserve the additional hold before and after producer
retirement. A pending wait requires a multiplier-condition Decode branch,
absent Address/cache/loop/suppression and either actual multiplier Execute or
no Execute after retirement. Malformed shapes reject. Validation checks
represented resources, not absent historical instructions or a board/disk image.

sharc_multiplier_branch_stalls checks a literal nine-column five-stage oracle:
Table4-55 extended by case2's extra hold, with actual multiply/branch/target
retirement. Every phase resumes against independent RAM/CPU/fetch before and
after Execute retirement. Captured producer/branch words survive actual RAM
replacement by invalid words. A fixed 1152-case matrix covers four multiplier
conditions, four PE sign combinations, SISD/SIMD and SISD parallel old-value
copies, four branch kinds, both DB modes and zero/one/two guest NOPs. Literal
truth bits, products, DB-slot increments and native CALL/RTS frames are checked.
Twelve additional SISD/SIMD positive-zero cases check MV/MS/complements and no
false dependency for TRUE or retained EQ.

Unknown operands preserve the failed producer/wait and allow repaired captured
execution. The second hold needs no ASTAT value; a later unknown Address
decision fails atomically until repaired. False MV returns need no stack.
Actual cache conflict, wrong bus and foreign SYSCTL transaction fail without
consuming the wait; repaired clocks continue. Malformed snapshots check absent/
wrong Decode/Execute, Address, suppression, loop and cache phases. The corrected
two-clock expectation in sharc_floating_multiply failed against eb0be72 before
this implementation. No checks were weakened; no fuzz inputs.
